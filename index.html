Stress How the body responds to challenging situations. ›

What it is

Stress is the body's natural response to situations that feel challenging, demanding, or unfamiliar.

What causes it

  • Academic or work pressure
  • Financial difficulties
  • Relationship problems
  • Health issues
  • Life changes or transitions
  • Lack of sleep or rest

Signs to notice

  • Constant worry or irritability
  • Headaches or muscle tension
  • Fatigue and difficulty sleeping
  • Trouble concentrating
  • Changes in appetite
  • Feeling overwhelmed

Ways to cope

  • Practice deep breathing or relaxation exercises.
  • Organize tasks and take regular breaks.
  • Exercise and maintain a consistent sleep schedule.
  • Talk to someone you trust.
  • Seek professional help if stress becomes difficult to manage.
Anxiety Worry, nervousness, and uneasiness explained. ›

What it is

Anxiety can involve feelings of worry, nervousness, fear, or uneasiness, especially when facing uncertainty or perceived threats.

What causes it

  • Academic or work pressure
  • Difficult life experiences
  • Health or financial concerns
  • Relationship problems
  • Major changes or uncertainty
  • Lack of sleep or excessive caffeine

Signs to notice

  • Constant worry or fear
  • Restlessness or irritability
  • Rapid heartbeat or shortness of breath
  • Muscle tension
  • Difficulty concentrating or sleeping
  • Avoiding certain people, places, or activities

Ways to cope

  • Practice slow breathing or grounding exercises.
  • Challenge unhelpful thoughts and focus on what you can control.
  • Maintain regular sleep, exercise, and meal routines.
  • Limit caffeine and take breaks from stressful activities.
  • Talk to someone you trust.
  • Seek professional help if anxiety persists or interferes with daily life.
Burnout Recognizing prolonged exhaustion. ›

What it is

Burnout can happen when prolonged demands leave someone feeling emotionally or mentally exhausted.

What causes it

  • Heavy or ongoing schoolwork
  • Constant pressure or high expectations
  • Not getting enough rest
  • Trying to balance too many responsibilities
  • Lack of time for yourself
  • Feeling like your efforts aren't making progress

Signs to notice

  • Feeling constantly tired or drained
  • Difficulty concentrating
  • Losing motivation to study or complete tasks
  • Feeling easily irritated or frustrated
  • Feeling overwhelmed by responsibilities
  • Wanting to withdraw from activities you usually do

Ways to cope

  • Take regular breaks from schoolwork
  • Break large tasks into smaller steps
  • Get enough sleep and rest
  • Make time for enjoyable activities
  • Avoid trying to finish everything at once
  • Talk with someone you trust when things become difficult
Emotional Well-being Understanding and managing your emotions. ›

What it is

Emotional well-being involves understanding your feelings, responding to challenges, and finding healthy ways to cope.

Why it matters

Emotions can provide information about how we are experiencing situations and can help us recognize when something may need attention.

Ways to manage it

  • Recognizing your feelings
  • Giving yourself time to process emotions
  • Expressing your feelings appropriately
  • Talking to someone you trust
  • Making time for activities you enjoy
Healthy Coping Finding constructive ways to handle challenges. ›

What it is

Healthy coping means using constructive ways to respond to difficult feelings, situations, or stress.

Examples

  • Talking to someone you trust
  • Taking breaks
  • Organizing responsibilities
  • Journaling
  • Relaxing
  • Engaging in activities you enjoy

Why it matters

Healthy coping strategies can help you manage challenges and give you opportunities to recover when things become stressful.

When to reach out

You don't have to wait until things feel overwhelming.

Consider talking to someone when stress, worry, sadness, or other difficulties become hard to manage or begin to interfere with everyday life — school, sleep, relationships, or things you usually enjoy.

Reaching out early is not a last resort. It's a reasonable first step, and asking for help is a sign of self-awareness, not weakness.
